Spirit Camera: The Cursed Memoir

In honor of Halloween, I wanted to play something quick and festive from my library. Of my options, I landed on Spirit Camera: The Cursed Memoir - which I knew to be an AR based game that leveraged the physical manual. At only a few hours in duration I figured it was worth a try, even if it sounded a little janky. What I didn't realize, is that the story is an entry in the Fatal Frame series entitled The Diary of Faces. I have heard of Fatal Frame before, as a less popular horror series with some parallels to Silent Hill, but I never figured my first foray with it would be through a camera-based novelty game. The story begins pretty abruptly with you finding a camera, and mysterious diary - perhaps alluding to the "finding" of the manual in the game case. Either way, you use the camera on the manual and the environment around you in order to unveil the secrets of the diary, and of the companion who pops out if it when you start the main story. The main narrative is short and not terribly scary, but has an ambient horror that is characteristic of the J-Horror genre. Aside from the various AR puzzles presented in the manual, there are recurring combat sections that test your aptitude with the camera. You are tasked with photographing spirits as they bounce around the room and attack you - a novel experience even if it was a bit grating. Outside of the main story, which culminates in a nice twist and battle, there are a handful of other game modes. Some are just simple camera filters and AR photo ops, but there are a few mini-games that add some additional ways to play outside of what the story presents. Overall Spirit Camera: The Cursed Memoir is an oddity of a game, and one that we probably won't see in the future as integrated cameras have disappeared from video game consoles.

Play it for the:

  • Quirky use of the camera
  • J-Horror story
  • Use of the physical manual for puzzles
