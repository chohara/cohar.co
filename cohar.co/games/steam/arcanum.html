Arcanum: Of Steamworks and Magick Obscura

Arcanum: Of Steamworks and Magick Obscura is a CRPG that was released in 2001, and has since garnered a niche cultlike following. It was also this months selection for my video gaming book club - Joystick Junkies! Like many games that were developed for older operating systems, Arcanum basically necessitates an unofficial community patch in order to make the game playable. In this case, the fan patch makes bug fixes and mechanical changes in addition to the usual compatibility fixes. I'm always conflicted on this, because I like to contextualize most games I play as though they were just released - otherwise it would be totally unfair to compare a GBA title to one on the PS5. Some of the enhancements provided for in the patch are major sources of relief, and in some cases fixed quest lines that were completely broken on launch. Even with the fixes the game is jarring to play, and it may be too off-putting for someone who isn't experienced with antique games. The gameplay is fairly typical for a CRPG with an isometric perspective and point-and-click navigation, but more comparable to Fallout 2 than it is to Baldur's Gate 3. Where this game shines, and where it remains rather timeless, is in it's setting and how the setting reflects your build. In Arcanum magic and technology coexist, although in conflict with one another. Technology - mostly championed by Dwarves - is characterized by industrial age technologies like trains, guns, and engines. Magic, which is of Elven interest, is fairly typical for a fantasy world. You are given the option to develop your character toward whichever ideology you see fit, but be warned that characters will judge you based on your proclivities. Become too proficient in magic, and you won't be permitted to riding the train. On the other side, if you become too fascinated with technology then you won't be able to cast magic at all. The world is crafted around this dynamic, and it plays out in a very interesting and creative way. In 2026 (or beyond) you'll be playing this game for this aspect, along with the unique build options it presents. I typically like to play a simple brutish barbarian (named Cohar, of course) with my first run through any CRPG and this time was no different. I went with a half-ogre with the rare half-ogre birth background, which conveniently avoided some of the pitfalls that come with playing an off-race. Characters will react harshly to you if they don't like your race, you are too ugly, or too stupid. Every NPC seems to have some prejudice, and this can ultimately lead to you being locked out of certain content depending on your initial character selection. If you understand this going in, it can add for some unique worldbuilding, but otherwise it can totally sour your experience. Irritations plague Arcanum, but despite that it remains capable of sharing a unique world with some archaic but foundational gameplay to go along with it.

Play it for the:

  • Unique setting where technology and magic clash
  • Primitive CRPG experience
  • Rampant racism
