Fire Emblem: Radiant Dawn

To begin, I want to mention the odyssey that went into playing Fire Emblem: Radiant Dawn. I bought a physical copy of this game a long while ago, but never got to play it - mostly because I had never played Path of Radiance before. When I finally got around to trying the disc, it seemed to be smooth, until I tried promoting a unit about seven hours into my playthrough. The cutscene that loaded resulted in a disc read error, and try as I might I couldn't fix it. This is especially a problem because there are multiple forced promotions in the game which are unavoidable. Eventually I decided to try having the disc resurfaced - something that basically no one does anymore. Thankfully, Videogamesnewyork in NYC still offers the service! Fran and I stopped by on one of our trips to the city, and gave it a shot. The staff mentioned that it may not be repairable due to a small, deep puncture on the disc, but they said it was worth trying anyway. Once we got home, I immediately popped the disc into my Wii U and tried to promote a unit. I held my breath, and the promotion cutscene finally loaded! Shout out to Videogamesnewyork for salvaging this review, and my copy of Radiant Dawn!!
Over the past couple years I've been playing through some of Fire Emblem titles that I missed in anticipation of the new entry, Fortune's Weave. With my recent completion of Path of Radiance, the natural next step was to play Radiant Dawn. Truthfully, I wasn't expecting all that much from this title, as I had always heard more fanfare about the aforementioned Path of Radiance. I was quickly enticed by a couple things: the quickened pace of gameplay, and the tactical difficulty - even on "Normal" mode. While somewhat unforgiving, I appreciate the emphasis on the tactics element of gameplay in the Fire Emblem series. From the first chapter of the first part, each map feels thoughtfully designed for an engaging tactical experience. Narrative relevance is often emphasized too, as battles take place in unique and memorable settings - or with unusual objectives. Radiant Dawn is a fantastic culmination of the myriad characters throughout the continent of Tellius, even though the narrative threads can get a bit tangled at points. The story begins following the Dawn Brigade, a group of ruffians led by Micaiah, who turns out to be a prophet of sorts with a name straight from the Old Testament. From there you embark on a journey of conflict across the continent of Tellius, from the back alleys in a city, to ultimately challenging Gods. Along the way you swap control of different units and armies persistently, leaving you in command of the Greil Mercenaries, or even the Crimean Royal Knights. Some players have reported a feeling of whiplash with this characteristic, as they couldn't get settled with a single group. I felt the opposite - the chapter-to-chapter changes made for a unique tactics puzzle each time, while also demonstrating the nature of a continental conflict in which multiple factions are acting simultaneously. Like I mentioned earlier, the narrative definitely suffers as a result overall, but in a way it feels fitting. History isn't always so clear cut, and Radiant Dawn feels a lot like an epic historical recount of a lost continent. I very much enjoyed this game, and to me it made Path of Radiance worth it. It was especially enjoyable seeing all of the familiar faces from across Tellius reappear throughout the playthrough. As a final note, the trailers for the upcoming Fortune's Weave have me excited! I'm itching for a similar experience, and I'm optimistic it will deliver given what's been revealed.

Play it for the:

  • Precise tactics and strategy gameplay
  • Epic conclusion to the Tellius Saga
  • Growth and evolution of Ike
