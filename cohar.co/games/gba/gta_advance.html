Grand Theft Auto Advance

I am writing this review on the release date of Grand Theft Auto VI - the long awaited next iteration into the iconic GTA series. While I have barely yet to play, it seems to be living up to lofty expectations. It has also been an immense contrast to Grand Theft Auto Advance - which I completed just as VI launched at 12:05AM. Oddly enough, Advance released on the same day as Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas, so it has been an afterthought since the day it came out. Played from the top down perspective and taking place in Liberty City - GTA Advance is a highly filtered version of the GTA III experience, although it boasts a standalone prequel storyline. The game follows the now typical formula which has you dealing with quirky characters to accomplish varies criminal tasks. Most tasks here boil down to driving somewhere and standing in a blue circle (or killing people) to advance the mission. The map is hard to parse on the GBA screen and the map is hard to follow, although it gets easier as comfortability builds. Interestingly, you get to unlock new areas like other titles so you end up with a decently sized map to explore, even if it is a bit sparse. Scattered throughout are classic locations such as the police station, hospital, and Ammu-nation, along with rampage events and hidden collectibles called "packages". I focused on the main story, as the side content offers little in the way of emergent gameplay outside of killing and driving. The characters are quirky in a way that is iconic to the GTA franchise, but the story is told entirely through splash art with dialog boxes. The main highlight in the gameplay is ultimately the driving, which feels somewhat rewarding as you get better at dodging other vehicles and using back-alleys. The weapon suite is questionable, with the shotgun being overwhelmingly the best gun while everything else palls in comparison. The machine guns (which account for most of the weapon choices) are extremely weak and tough to use as you are limited to aiming in eight cardinal directions. One mission in particular - Smackdown - basically necessitates the use of the shotgun, as the other weapons are simply not fast enough at killing to complete the objective in the alloted time. Performance wise they did an adequate job given the limited hardware of the GBA, but there are still notable frame drops during busy cop encounters, or when trying to use an automatic weapon. Overall Grand Theft Auto Advance is a quirky piece of history in the storied GTA series, and one that will probably be forgotten as time goes on. Give it a try if you want to see where it all started!

Play it for the:

  • History of Grand Theft Auto
  • Liberty City lore
  • Hardest mission in the franchise (Smackdown)
